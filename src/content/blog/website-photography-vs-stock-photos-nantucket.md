**Short answer:** stock photography costs Nantucket businesses trust. Visitors recognize the island, and a generic beach or a model in a chef's coat reads as fake immediately. A half-day shoot of your real place, people, and products produces images for your website, Google profile, and social media for years, and it is usually the highest-return part of a web project. If you already own good photos, use them. If you do not, get them before you spend on design.

## What stock costs you

Stock photos are cheap to buy and expensive to use.

**They look like everyone else.** The same licensed images appear on hundreds of sites. A visitor who saw that sunset on a competitor's site an hour ago now trusts you less.

**They are recognizably not here.** Nantucket's light, architecture, and landscape are specific. Grey shingles, cobblestones, hydrangeas, the flat horizon off Cisco. Visitors who have been here once know it. A stock image of a Caribbean beach on a Nantucket rental site undermines every claim on the page.

**Google notices originality.** Search engines and AI tools increasingly favor pages with original images, a named author, and evidence of real experience. Stock imagery is the opposite of that signal.

**They cannot show your product.** Nobody has a stock photo of your clam chowder, your boat, your paintings, or your crew.

## What a half-day shoot should cover

A focused half day in good light gets everything most island businesses need. This is the shot list I work from.

- **The place.** Exterior from the street, the entrance, and two or three interior angles. Early morning or late afternoon light.
- **The people.** The owner, the team, and a candid or two of them working. Real faces are the most trusted images on any site.
- **The product.** Food, drinks, products, rooms, boats, finished landscapes. Shot in context, not on a white background, unless you are selling online.
- **Details.** Hands at work, textures, signage, the things that make your place yours.
- **The context.** One or two frames that place you on the island. The harbor behind the boat, the cobblestones out front, the moors beyond the property.

That produces around 30 to 60 finished images. Enough for the website, a full Google Business Profile, a few months of social posts, and print if you need it.

## Golden hour by location

Timing on Nantucket is straightforward because the island is flat and the light is clean.

- **Downtown and the wharves** photograph best in early morning before the crowds, with soft light on the brick and shingle.
- **South shore beaches and Cisco** are afternoon and sunset locations.
- **Madaket** is the sunset spot, and everyone knows it, so arrive early.
- **Brant Point and the harbor** work at both ends of the day. Sunrise over the jetties is underused.
- **'Sconset** is a morning location, with the bluff and the rose-covered cottages facing east.

Interiors can be shot any time with the right approach, which is usually turning off the overhead lights and using the windows.

## Phone photos: when they are fine

Your phone is fine for daily social posts, gallery updates, and before-and-after shots. It is fine for the weekly Google Business Profile upload. Hold it level, shoot in good light, and do not use the zoom.

Where phone photos fall short is the hero images: the homepage, the top of each service page, the about page portrait. Those images are seen by every visitor and set the tone for everything. Spend on those once.

## Rights and reuse

When you hire a photographer, agree on usage in writing. You should be able to use the images on your website, social media, Google, print, and advertising without additional fees. If you hire me, that is the default. Every image I shoot for a client's site is theirs to use.

If you use stock anyway, download and self-host the file, keep a record of the license, and never hotlink from the stock site. Free stock licenses from Unsplash and Pexels allow commercial use, but they do not include model or property releases, and the images are still generic.

## Planning it into the project

The best time to shoot is before the design starts, so the site is built around the real images rather than placeholders. In practice, that means shooting in the shoulder season or the weeks after Stroll, while the business still looks alive and the light is good.
