**Short answer:** to close a Nantucket business for the winter on Google, mark it as temporarily closed or set special hours, never as permanently closed. Update your hours before you reopen, post an announcement, add fresh photos, and ask for a few reviews in the first weeks. Done right, your profile keeps its ranking through the off-season and is ready to convert the first ferry of spring.

## The mistake that costs a season

Google lets customers suggest edits to business profiles. When a visitor walks up to your locked door in February and sees a "closed for the season" sign, some of them helpfully report the business as closed. If you have not told Google otherwise, it may mark you permanently closed, and that label follows you into April. Customers see a red "Permanently closed" notice, stop clicking, and Google's confidence in your profile drops.

The fix takes five minutes and is the most valuable five minutes of your off-season.

## Special hours vs temporary closure

You have two tools.

**Special hours** are for short, specific changes. Closed on Thanksgiving, open late for Stroll weekend, closed the Tuesday after Columbus Day. Set them for each date and Google shows the right hours on the right day.

**Temporarily closed** is for the winter. It tells Google and customers that you are closed now and will be back. Your profile stays live, your reviews and photos stay visible, and searchers see "Temporarily closed" instead of a red warning. When you reopen, you flip it back and set your regular hours.

Do not delete your hours to signal closure. A profile with no hours looks abandoned. Do not mark yourself permanently closed. Reversing that requires a re-verification that can take weeks.

## Step by step: closing for the season

1. Sign in to your Google Business Profile.
2. Under hours, set the business as temporarily closed. Add the expected reopening date if you know it.
3. Write a short update post: "Closed for the season after Stroll. Reopening for Daffodil weekend in April. Gift cards available online." Posts stay visible and reassure people.
4. Update your website and Instagram bio to say the same thing.
5. Set an email reminder for six weeks before you plan to reopen.

## Step by step: reopening

1. Two weeks before opening, switch the profile back to open and enter your regular hours, including any shoulder-season schedule.
2. Post an announcement with the opening date and anything new.
3. Upload a handful of new photos. Fresh images signal an active business and get shown in search.
4. Check that your phone number, website, and menu or services link still work. Things break over the winter.
5. In the first two weeks, ask happy customers for reviews. A few new reviews in April boost visibility for the whole season.

## Photos and posts through the season

Google profiles with regular photo uploads and posts get more views and clicks. It does not have to be much. One photo a week from May through October, and a post whenever something changes: a new menu, a Fourth of July schedule, a Figawi special. Take the photos with your phone and upload them from the app.

## Questions and answers

Your profile has a Q&A section that anyone can post to. Seed it yourself with the questions you get every day. Do you take reservations, is there parking, can I bring a dog, are you open in the off-season. Answer them clearly. It saves you calls and gives Google more to work with.

## Service-area businesses

If you come to the customer, such as landscapers, caretakers, cleaners, photographers, and caterers, do not show a home address. Set up as a service-area business, hide the address, and list the areas you serve: Nantucket, Siasconset, Madaket, and any off-island towns if you travel. Showing an address where customers cannot visit is a common reason profiles get suspended.

## Match your website

Whatever you set on Google, your website should say the same thing. Hours in the footer of every page, and a one-line seasonal notice on the homepage during the closure. A mismatch between the two is the most common source of bad reviews I see for island businesses.
