**Short answer:** local SEO for a Nantucket business comes down to four things. A complete and accurate Google Business Profile. Consistent name, address, and phone number across the directories that matter here. A website whose page titles, headings, and image descriptions say what you do and where. And a steady flow of real reviews. Do those four and you will outrank most of the island, because most of the island has not done them.

## Why Nantucket is different

Search volume here is small compared with a city, but the people searching are ready to spend. A visitor typing "Nantucket bike rental" from the ferry is renting a bike in the next hour. A homeowner searching "Nantucket caretaker" in January is hiring for the year. That intent is why a little effort goes a long way.

The other difference is that your competition is thin. Many island businesses run on a Facebook page and word of mouth. That is fine until a visitor searches, finds three competitors and not you, and books elsewhere.

## Step one: Google Business Profile

This is the most important thing on the list, and it is free.

- Claim or create your profile at business.google.com.
- Choose the most specific primary category. "Seafood restaurant" beats "Restaurant". "Landscaper" beats "Contractor".
- Fill in every field. Hours, phone, website, services, attributes, description.
- Upload real photos. Your storefront, your team, your work, your food. Google shows these in search results and on maps, and profiles with photos get far more clicks.
- Set seasonal hours properly. My [guide to seasonal hours on Google](/blog/google-business-profile-seasonal-hours-nantucket) explains how to close for the winter without being marked permanently closed.
- If you serve customers at their homes rather than a storefront, set up as a service-area business and list Nantucket, Siasconset, and Madaket as areas served.

## Step two: citations

Citations are listings of your business name, address, and phone number on other sites. Google uses them to confirm you are real and where you are. The rule is that they must match exactly. "Main St" on one and "Main Street" on another is a small problem that adds up.

For a Nantucket business, the listings that matter most:

- **Nantucket Island Chamber of Commerce** member directory. It ranks on the first page for many island searches, so a listing there is both a citation and a direct source of customers.
- **Yelp**, which ranks well for Nantucket service searches whether you like it or not.
- **Apple Business Connect** and **Bing Places**, because iPhones and Windows machines default to them.
- **Facebook** business page with the same details.
- Industry directories for your field, such as TripAdvisor for restaurants and charters or Houzz for landscapers.

Check each one, fix mismatches, and add your website link.

## Step three: on-page basics

Your website tells Google what you do through a few specific places. Most template sites leave them blank or generic.

- **Page title.** Every page needs a unique title that says what the page is and where. "Sunset Sails from Nantucket Harbor | Captain Jane Charters" not "Home".
- **Meta description.** One or two sentences that would make someone click. Mention the island once, naturally.
- **One main heading per page** that matches the title's meaning.
- **Image alt text.** Describe what is actually in the photo. "Lobster roll on the deck at our Straight Wharf restaurant" is useful. "Nantucket restaurant best lobster roll Nantucket" is spam and Google treats it that way.
- **Structured data.** A block of code that tells Google your business type, address, hours, and services. It is invisible to visitors and invaluable to search engines. This is one of the things I set up on every site I build.
- **Speed.** A slow site ranks worse and converts worse. Compress your images.

Do not create separate pages for "Nantucket", "ACK", "Siasconset", and "Madaket" with the same text. Google calls that doorway abuse and penalizes it.

## Step four: reviews

Reviews drive both ranking and clicks. The process is simple and most businesses skip it.

- Ask every happy customer, in person or by text, with a direct link to your Google review page.
- Ask at the moment of satisfaction. After the trip, after the meal, after the lawn looks perfect.
- Respond to every review, good or bad, briefly and kindly.
- Never buy reviews or write your own. The Federal Trade Commission now fines businesses for fake reviews, and Google removes them.

## Island keywords worth targeting

People search Nantucket with a specific vocabulary. Use it naturally in your content.

- "Nantucket" plus your service is the core. "Nantucket photographer", "Nantucket landscaper", "Nantucket web design".
- Neighborhood names for location-specific businesses. 'Sconset, Madaket, Surfside, Cisco, Brant Point, Mid-Island.
- Event names for seasonal offers. Stroll, Daffodil Festival, Figawi, Fourth of July.
- "ACK" appears in searches from people who know the island. Mention it once if it fits.

## What about AI search

Google's AI Overviews and tools like ChatGPT increasingly answer local questions directly. They pull from the same sources: your Google profile, your website, and consistent listings. Sites with clear, direct answers, named authors, and real photos get cited more. Everything above helps here too. There is no separate trick.
