**Short answer:** most Nantucket landscaping, caretaking, and property services companies get their best clients from second-home owners who live off-island and search from a desk in the winter. Those owners want proof you are reliable when they are not there: real photos of real properties, clear services, how you report and communicate, and reviews from people like them. A website written for that reader wins the accounts that actually pay the bills.

## Who is really searching

Nantucket's real estate market is dominated by high-value homes, many owned by people who spend a few weeks a year on the island. In 2025 the median single-family sale price passed $3.5 million, and homes above $5 million made up a large share of the dollar volume. The people who own those houses need someone to open them in May, close them in October, check them after a nor'easter, and keep the hydrangeas alive in between.

They are not driving past your truck. They are typing "Nantucket caretaking" or "landscaper Nantucket" into Google in January, comparing three websites, and calling the one that looks like it will still be in business in five years.

Your year-round neighbors already know you. The website is for everyone else.

## Service pages that convert

A single "services" page with a bulleted list does not answer the off-island owner's questions. Give each core service its own short page:

- **Landscape maintenance.** What a weekly visit includes, how you handle the season opening and closing, and how you communicate when something needs attention.
- **Caretaking and house checks.** How often you check, what you look for, what happens when you find a leak, and how the owner hears about it.
- **Storm response.** What you do before and after a hurricane or winter storm. This page gets read in September.
- **Design and installation.** Hardscape, plantings, irrigation. Show finished projects.
- **Seasonal open and close.** A page for this alone, because it is a specific search.

On each page, say what you do, show a photo of you doing it, and tell them how to get a quote. Keep the tone plain. Off-island owners are hiring a professional, not reading a brochure.

## Before and after galleries

Landscaping is visual work, and your website should show it. A gallery of before and after pairs from real island properties is the most persuasive content you can publish. Name the neighborhood if the client is fine with it. 'Sconset bluff, Polpis, Madaket, Cliff Road. Owners searching for a landscaper near their house will recognize it.

Take the photos on the same day of the year, in similar light, from the same spot. Phone photos are fine for the gallery if they are sharp and level. For the hero images on your homepage and service pages, a proper shoot is worth doing once. Those photos become your Google Business Profile images, your truck decals, and your estimates. I cover the approach in [website photography vs stock](/blog/website-photography-vs-stock-photos-nantucket).

## Communication is a feature

The thing that separates a good caretaking company from a great one is reporting. If you send photo updates after every visit, say so on the website and show an example. If you have an owner portal, show it. If you text the owner a picture of the house after every storm, that is worth a paragraph on the homepage.

Off-island owners are paying for peace of mind. Show them what it looks like.

## Reviews and referrals

Reviews from other off-island owners are worth more than anything you can write about yourself. Ask your best clients for a Google review in the fall when the season is done and they are grateful. Put three of them on the site with the client's first name and the neighborhood.

Referrals from real estate agents, builders, and property managers are the other channel. Make it easy for them to send someone your way: a clean site, a fast contact form, and a phone number that gets answered.
