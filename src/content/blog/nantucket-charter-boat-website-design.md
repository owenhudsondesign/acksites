**Short answer:** a charter website converts when it answers the trust questions first. Who is the captain, what is the boat, where do we meet, how much is it, and what happens if the weather turns. Show real photos of the real boat and the real captain, publish your rates, explain deposits and cancellations plainly, and make it possible to book or at least request a date from a phone. Charters that hide their prices lose to charters that do not.

## What guests check first

I grew up around this harbor, and I have watched families on Straight Wharf comparing charter options on their phones. They are not reading your mission statement. They are looking for:

- **A photo of the captain.** A real person, on the boat, smiling. This is the single most trust-building image on the site.
- **The boat itself.** Length, type, how many it carries, whether there is a head, shade, and a cooler. Photos from the dock and from on the water.
- **Where to meet.** Slip number, wharf name, and parking or walking directions from the ferry. Visitors do not know the difference between Straight Wharf and Old South Wharf, so tell them.
- **The price.** Half day, full day, sunset cruise, per boat or per person, what is included. If you do not list it, they assume it is expensive and move on.
- **The weather policy.** What happens if it blows. Refund, reschedule, or captain's call. Write it down.

## Pages that do the work

A charter site does not need to be big. Five pages done well beats fifteen done badly.

**Home.** One great photo, one sentence about what you do, a rates summary, and a book button. Hours or season dates in the footer.

**Trips.** One section per offering. Inshore fishing, offshore, sunset sails, family trips, private events. Each with duration, capacity, what to bring, and price.

**The boat and captain.** Your story, your licenses, how many seasons you have run out of Nantucket. Guests want to know they are in experienced hands in Nantucket Sound.

**Book.** A request form at minimum, with date, trip type, group size, and phone number. A real booking system with deposits if you run enough trips to justify it.

**FAQ.** Seasickness, what to wear, whether kids can come, fish cleaning, tipping, the ferry schedule. Every question you answer here is a phone call you do not have to take at 6am.

## Online booking and deposits

The choice is between a request form and a live booking system.

A request form is free and works well if you run a few trips a week and like to talk to guests before confirming. The downside is that guests booking at 11pm from Connecticut want an answer now, and some will book elsewhere while waiting.

A live booking system, such as FareHarbor, Peek, or Xola, shows real availability, takes a deposit, and sends confirmations automatically. It costs a per-booking fee or subscription but fills the calendar while you are on the water. For most Nantucket charters running daily in season, it pays for itself by July.

Whichever you choose, take a deposit. A deposit turns a maybe into a booking, and it protects you when a family cancels the morning of a perfect day.

## Photos and video

Your phone is fine for a lot of this if you use it well. Shoot at the beginning and end of the day when the light is low. Get the boat under way from another boat if you can. Photograph guests holding fish with their permission and their smiles. A thirty-second phone video of the boat leaving the harbor at sunset does more than a paragraph.

For the hero photos, the captain portrait, and the boat at the dock, a proper shoot is worth it. Those images run on your site, your Google Business Profile, your Instagram, and every directory for years. I shoot these as part of most charter site projects. ## Figawi and peak-week pages

Figawi weekend in late May brings hundreds of boats and thousands of sailors to the island, and it is the unofficial start of the season. Fourth of July week and the August peak are the other moments people search for charters months ahead. A short page for each, with availability and any special trips, catches those searches. Publish them in February, before the planning starts.
